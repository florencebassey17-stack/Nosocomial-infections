# Nosocomial-infections
# 1. Introduction

Nosocomial infections are healthcare-associated infections. They are picked up during care. They were not present when the person was admitted.

Common types include:

- central-line associated bloodstream infection
- catheter-associated urinary tract infection
- surgical site infection
- ventilator-associated pneumonia
- *Clostridioides difficile* infection

The idea to keep: **the patient did not walk in with this infection. Something in care helped it in.**

## 3. Key words

- **Nosocomial / HAI** — infection associated with healthcare
- **Admission** — the point we use to ask: was this already there?
- **Opportunistic** — causes disease more easily when defences are low
- **Device-associated** — linked to a line, catheter, ventilator, or wound

## 4. Body

### What it is

Nosocomial infections, also called healthcare-associated infections, are acquired during healthcare delivery. They were not present before admission.

### What usually causes them

**Bacteria** — the most common group. Many are opportunistic. They cause trouble when the immune system is low or a device gives them a way in.  
Examples: *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Streptococcus* spp., *Enterococcus* spp., Enterobacteriaceae, *Escherichia coli*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.

**Fungi** — usually opportunistic, especially in immunocompromised people.  
Examples: *Candida* spp.; *Aspergillus fumigatus* (often airborne).

**Viruses** — less common in this story, but they can be acquired in a healthcare setting.  
Examples: hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and HIV, especially when needles are unsafe.

### Example

A patient was admitted with fractured bones. She did not come in with an infection. During her stay she was given an intramuscular injection. After two to three weeks she became ill. A blood culture grew *Staphylococcus*. She had staphylococcal bacteraemia (sepsis).

A possible cause: the syringe or the technique was contaminated before the injection went into her body.

## 5. Common mix-up

“She is in hospital, so any infection is nosocomial.”  
Not automatically. The question is: **was it already there at admission?** If it started after care began, it belongs in this lesson.
